# Research Workflow

Use when the user requests web research or the answer depends on current, unstable, niche, or
externally documented facts.

## Research Question

Define the decision the research must support. Break broad topics into a small set of questions;
do not browse without a stopping condition.

## Source Order

1. Official documentation, specifications, standards, maintainers, and original authors.
2. Primary research or first-party engineering publications.
3. High-quality independent analysis for experience, tradeoffs, or disagreement.
4. Community discussion only for lived experience or discovery, clearly labeled as such.

For technical claims, prefer current primary sources. Check publication/update dates and product
versions. Open the supporting page; do not rely on search snippets.

## Synthesis

- Separate source-backed fact, source opinion, and your inference.
- Reconcile contradictions instead of counting sources.
- Adapt advice to the repository rather than importing generic architecture blindly.
- Keep direct quotations short and respect source licenses/copyright.
- Cite each important claim near where it is used.

Stop when additional sources no longer change the recommendation or its confidence. Record sources
and review date in durable workflow documents when the research informs persistent rules.
